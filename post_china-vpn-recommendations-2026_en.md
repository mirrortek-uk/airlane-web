## Why is bypassing the wall so hard?

The GFW combines IP blacklists, DNS poisoning, deep packet inspection and active probing — and it evolves continuously. Older protocols (raw Shadowsocks, PPTP, default OpenVPN) carry obvious fingerprints. Modern anti-censorship leans on TLS-mimicking or UDP-based protocols: VLESS+Reality, Trojan, Hysteria2, TUIC. Obfuscation and protocol freshness matter more than raw bandwidth.

### Why is my VPN slow?

Four usual suspects:

1. **Evening peak congestion** — international gateways are limited; 20:00–23:00 is the worst window, and carriers may throttle encrypted-looking traffic (QoS)
2. **Old protocols** — outdated clients/protocols get DPI-flagged; prefer Trojan/VLESS/Hysteria2
3. **Detoured landing routes** — your "US" node may actually relay through Germany or Singapore ("hearse nodes"); good providers use BGP relay or IPLC private lines
4. **Your own hardware** — proxy plugins on an old router can max its CPU; enable AES-NI or get a beefier box

Rule of thumb: **fresh protocol + stable route + off-peak usage** ≈ near-native experience.

**Summary: self-hosting is a rabbit hole.** Unless you enjoy 24/7 cat-and-mouse with the GFW, a mature subscription is the sane choice — spend the money, save the time.

## VPN won't connect? Do this

### 1. Protocol first — if it fails, switch

Old protocols get flagged fastest. Move to Trojan/VLESS/Hysteria2 or the provider's recommended "China" mode.

### 2. Switch nodes — not necessarily popular ones

Popular nodes get the most attention. Try less obvious regions.

### 3. Check time sync — many people miss this

TLS-based auth can fail if your clock drifts. Sync system time.

### 4. Update the client

Blocking fixes ship in updates — an old version may be half-broken already.

### 5. Rule out the basics: local network & DNS

Restart the router, flush DNS, try a different network (mobile hotspot) to isolate.
